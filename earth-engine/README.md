# Earth Engine / 01 — geographic prototype

An **isolated prototype** for a much larger and more geographically honest achievement world. This is not wired into the homepage. A separate, access-protected Vercel **preview** exists for friend testing; the production portfolio at batikanor.com has not been changed. It treats Earth as the map instead of enlarging a small fictional board: planet → region → actual venue → one curated metre-scale driving demo.

```sh

npm install
npm run dev -- --port 4177
```

## What actually exists

- MapLibre GL JS globe and Mercator transition, real WGS84 coordinates, streamed tiled imagery, and Terrarium raster DEM terrain. We do not bundle a monolithic 3D Earth mesh.
- 32 original project stories from the deployed portfolio, reproduced verbatim from its source data: full descriptions, media in authored order, captions, evidence links, technologies and repository widget. The map has no invented project/sculpture titles or generated descriptions. Progressive geographic disclosure shows region → country → city → event points. The compact chronological navigator is latest-first with bounded previous/next, not grouped by city; arrows open the selected project immediately. The old separate Projects list is intentionally not duplicated.
- One prototype 3D scene—the Garching radar—has a physically scaled installation and a finite, user-triggered scan. It is **visual map art, not an actual surveyed structure or a claim about the original achievement**. The other 31 do not yet have authored 3D installations; future objects must be reviewed against the actual project rather than invented narrative.
- A 758 KB venue-only geometry tile from official Bavarian LoD2 open data adds 262 real building roof/wall forms near Garching at neighbourhood scale. A separate 2.39 MB derived atlas projects official DOP20 aerial imagery onto their roofs. Both are lazy-fetched, outside the initial JS bundle. The **facades remain untextured generalized surfaces**, not photogrammetry; they hide at venue/drive zoom to avoid giant blank walls occluding the installation. See the [reproducible converters and source details](design/garching-lod2.md).
- Procedurally modelled, physically scaled (4.34 m) touring car as a MapLibre/Three custom layer. Its prototype drive starts only at the manually inspected Garching paved campus access area, approximately `11.666954, 48.262269`. A tested bicycle-style controller gives smooth speed-sensitive steering, braking/reverse, drag, a look-ahead chase camera, and a 250 m local geofence. This is **not** a road-aware or collision-ready vehicle system. Other venues deliberately have DRIVE disabled so a car is not spawned on a pictured roof or obstacle.
- User-facing source attribution strip; imagery, relief, zoom and scale controls inside an accessible settings wheel. Garching has detailed orthophoto, while other places fall back to global imagery at the source's native resolution. [Project-content integration](design/project-content-integration.md) documents the authored data snapshot and media coverage.

The Bavarian high-detail raster is **not global**. MapLibre requests it only inside the explicitly declared Munich/Garching box `[11.1, 48.0, 11.9, 48.45]` above z12; either EOX or ESA/NASA sits below it according to the imagery switch. The box is a technical trial area, **not** a precise administrative boundary. Before release, replace this rectangular clip with true coverage polygons or per-country/city source adapters and test seams. Viewport-intersection logic conservatively shows Bavaria's credit where its imagery can appear.

`src/data/achievements.json` keeps map coordinates and titles but no invented landmark/story prose. `src/data/contestsAndActivities.js` is a verbatim snapshot of the deployed portfolio's author-written content. Treat venue geocodes and chosen ground anchors as **provisional**, not a verified public gazetteer.

## Visual / engineering caveats

- DOP20 resolves fine ground detail, but at z20+ it is **overzoomed** and visibly soft. Garching's LoD2 adds roof-textured 3D building masses at neighbourhood scale only; at close range the facades hide, so the surrounding roof shadows and trees in the orthophoto are flat. The physical-size car therefore demonstrates rendering, scale and basic handling, **not** a finished driving game. There is no navigation mesh, road graph, curb/roof avoidance, suspension or wheel-ground simulation, day/night consistency, or authored sound. The visible wheels *do* steer and rotate.
- The [Protective Radar installation](design/hero-venue.md) has distinctive geometry and a playable scan, but it is still a detailed procedural **blockout** rather than final authored Blender/PBR artwork. Its position is visually chosen beside the venue; it is not an actual real-world structure or surveyed placement.
- The Garching driving start is a curated spot on apparent paved access in the orthophoto, not a surveyed parking-space guarantee. Route/art validation remains necessary.
- Car rendering is purposefully 1:1 scale. The default [close chase](design/drive-camera-modes.md) at zoom 21.05 makes the car more legible; the C/touch toggle returns to a 20.15 overview. Both overzoom the z19 orthophoto, especially close chase, and cannot create missing ground pixels. The model is bespoke prototype geometry, not a production vehicle asset.
- On short mobile landscape canvases, the drive camera automatically pulls back. Drive temporarily pauses the 3D DEM/hillshade because MapLibre could render a black ground at street zoom in that aspect ratio; it restores the user's terrain preference on exit. The installation and car remain physically scaled on the local flat imagery during Drive.
- Outside the Bavaria trial box, event focus stops near z13.8 rather than grossly magnifying the ~10 m global mosaic to street scale. To deliver equally convincing local chapters elsewhere, add licensed regional orthophotos or city photogrammetry/3D Tiles, plus a multi-resolution asset selection policy.
- Tiles load online from their providers at runtime. Offline first visit is not supported; availability, rate limits, and imagery changes are outside this prototype's control. Do not silently mirror/rehost third-party imagery without confirming its terms.
- MapLibre + Three incur a meaningful JS/WebGL cost (the Vite build currently emits a large main chunk and a MapLibre worker). Production should dynamically import the drive layer, use scene budgets, profile low-end devices, and compare vector/PMTiles or self-hosted CDN cache approaches. A massive **world** does not imply a massive initial download: only visible tiles are requested.
- MapLibre currently logs `calculateFogMatrix is not supported on globe projection` once with this sky setting. It did not break rendering in local Chromium QA. The prior DEM `Marker.project` RangeError was eliminated by detaching invisible far-away DOM markers rather than merely hiding them.

## Path from proof to a proper game/package

1. Keep a **provider-neutral geographic core**: camera/LOD, terrain sampling, WGS84 metre stepping, tile adapters, credits, interaction pickers, and quality budgets. This is the plausible reusable npm package boundary.
2. Build a **content pipeline**: every achievement defines reviewed lat/lon, a safe ground anchor, model brief, mesh/texture LODs, affordance, animation, audio, attribution, and interaction acceptance tests. Do not generate 32 generic monuments.
3. Finish the existing **Garching vertical slice** to production art standards: author a PBR car and installation, a genuinely drivable road/parking segment, consistent shadows and ground-contact, collision and suspension, camera/chase tuning, controls, sound and accessibility. The current real-data/procedural slice is a foundation, not the finished chapter.
4. Expand the imagery registry by region under explicit licenses. Bavaria's OpenData orthophoto is a proof that realistic local detail can be streamed without shipping a giant client bundle; it is not a global rights/coverage solution.
5. If geography/game primitives stabilize, extract them into a versioned library with independently testable source adapters. Keep private achievement content and third-party assets outside the published package.

See the [three fidelity directions and recommendation](design/fidelity-options.md) and the [proposed package boundary and release gates](design/package-boundary.md). No npm package is published or promised by this experiment.
